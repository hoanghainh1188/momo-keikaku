- (idea by user) Biggest failure is B: the PM becomes a manual sync layer between the plan and where work actually happens; the tool assumes the PM is its only user
- (idea by coach) Invert the direction of travel: don't drag the team to the plan. Tool reads real signals (commits, ticket transitions, messages) and PROPOSES plan deltas; PM shifts from typist to approver
- (insight by user) Concrete failure: plans in MS Project, manually pushed out to Jira/Backlog/Asana, then manually pulled BACK into MS Project to measure progress and cost. A round trip, not a one-way sync. Multiple different trackers across projects
- (insight by user) Judgement on the return trip is unavoidable, not optional. Worse: trackers accumulate many tasks that were never in the plan — the two sides structurally diverge, not just drift
- (insight by user) Does BOTH, inconsistently: sometimes adds emergent tasks back into MS Project (which wrecks the baseline AND cascades into downstream task scheduling), sometimes silently omits them
- (insight by user) The hidden rule is task SIZE: work judged 'too small to bother recording' gets silently omitted
- (insight by coach) That omission is a one-directional BIAS, not random noise: actuals are always understated, never overstated. Future estimates are built on history that systematically excluded small work, so estimates stay wrong forever — a self-reinforcing loop. Design constraint: capturing a 20-minute task must cost under 20 seconds or the bias returns
- (technique) started One Feature Only
- (decision by user) One Feature Only pick: the plan-vs-reality reconciliation map (user endorsed the coach's bet rather than proposing a rival)
- (direction by user) Wants to return later to the classic set: WBS, Gantt, Resource, Calendar, Cost
- (idea by user) Monday-morning screen has THREE tiers: (1) how far the project has actually run, (2) where it is measurably diverging from reality, (3) where it MIGHT be diverging — suspected, not yet proven
- (idea by user) Tier-3 suspicion signals: (a) long-duration tasks whose reported progress is non-linear; (b) no visible output/deliverable at the milestones
- (insight by coach) Both of those signals are one disease: progress ASSERTED without evidence. The '% complete' field is the vulnerability — a self-reported number with nothing attached. PMI already prescribes the cure (8/80 work-package sizing + deliverable-based 0/100 progress); MS Project lets you ignore both. Product move: never accept naked % complete — show 'verified by evidence' vs 'claimed, unverified' as visually distinct
- (idea by user) Evidence definition: in this domain the output DOCUMENT is the evidence — design docs, minutes, research reports, specs cover nearly every task type
- (insight by coach) Gaming risk: if the tool DEMANDS evidence, people manufacture fake documents — paperwork theatre, the exact thing that makes PMI hated. Resolution principle: the tool never blocks and never punishes, it only makes truth visible. Absence of evidence is displayed as information, not as a violation. Corollary: attaching evidence must be near-automatic (tool watches repo/drive/tracker and links artifacts itself) or the 20-second constraint is breached and the bias returns
- (decision by user) Visibility: everyone on the team should see the divergence screen, but it must be configurable
- (insight by coach) Configurability does not remove the political dynamic, it relocates it to whoever configures. The DEFAULT setting is the product's real opinion and the de-facto policy: default-open = radical transparency (risk: fear and gaming); default-PM-only = back to MS Project's single-user assumption, the original disease
- (technique) started Assumption Reversal
- (insight by user) Reversal rejected as unworkable: in their reality the PM must always author the plan; bottom-up planning is not available
- (idea by coach) If the PM is the sole author, nobody but the PM committed to anything — so divergence is NOT a verdict on people, it is a measurement of the PLAN's accuracy. That defuses the visibility politics AND yields a new feature: score estimate accuracy per task type over time ('design tasks run 40% under, consistently'), giving the PM the feedback loop that breaks the under-reporting bias loop found earlier
- (insight by user) The PM-authors-the-plan rule is organizational PERCEPTION, not a contract constraint. Normal flow: PM drafts, then ratifies it with management and with the team
- (idea by coach) The ratification meeting already exists but leaves no durable trace. Capture it: each member confirms their own packages in one click, with optional dissent ('I think this is underestimated'). Gets bottom-up signal WITHOUT changing who authors the plan. Recorded dissent at planning time is probably the single strongest predictor for the tier-3 suspicion list
- (insight by user) Dissent does happen at ratification — some object, some correct — but the culture is mostly top-down, so it is sparse
- (idea by coach) In a top-down culture dissent is socially EXPENSIVE, therefore rare but high-precision — trust it more, don't try to increase its volume. Instead lower its cost: replace the binary objection with PMI three-point estimating (optimistic/likely/pessimistic). The SPREAD is dissent expressed in a form nobody can take offence at, it is quantitative so it feeds tier-3 directly, and it is PMI-canonical — fits the standardize-by-PMI goal exactly
- (insight by user) Objectors are sometimes right, sometimes wrong — dissent alone is not a reliable predictor
- (idea by coach) BIGGEST IDEA: nobody knows the hit rate because nobody ever measured it. Don't hard-code which tier-3 signals predict failure — RECORD every signal (long task, non-linear progress, missing deliverable, wide 3-point spread, dissent) and let outcomes SCORE them over time. The product becomes a signal-calibration engine tuned to this specific organization. DANGER: per-person calibration ('A is right 80% of the time') is a credibility score on humans — the surveillance risk returning worse. Default to scoring the SIGNAL, and keep any person-level calibration private to that person

- (insight by user) Deeper why for PMI: personal experience only covers frequently-encountered cases; PMI supplies the comprehensive theory needed to GENERALIZE beyond one's own sample
- (decision by user) PRIORITY REVERSAL: this is a product built TO SELL. Using it on his own team is dogfooding/validation, not the goal. The opening framing ('for us first, sell later') was the permission structure, not the real order
- (insight by coach) Consequence of the reversal: the calibration engine is a moat for long-time users but has a COLD-START problem for every new customer — day one there is no history, so every tier-3 signal is unweighted. PMI is what fills the gap before a customer's own data exists: ship PMI's priors as the starting weights, then let each org's outcomes retrain them. That makes the academic research load-bearing rather than decorative

- (idea by coach) Bugs are RETROACTIVE evidence that a completion claim was false: a task marked done that later spawns bug tickets proves the 'done' was premature. Rework is the SECOND hidden bias — effort spent fixing completed work is never attributed back to its origin task, so true cost is understated again (twin of the omitted-small-task bias from technique 1). Attributing rework back to the originating work package is something MS Project structurally cannot do, and it feeds calibration directly
