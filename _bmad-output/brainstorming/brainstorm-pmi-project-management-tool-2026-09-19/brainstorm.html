PLATE 01Technique · Failure Analysis

The round trip

Asked how the tool could fail hardest, the answer was not a feature. It was a description of a Tuesday: the project manager as a human sync layer, carrying plan and reality back and forth by hand.

  • — technique started: Failure Analysis. Stance switched to Facilitator, then back to Creative Partner: “I want you creating alongside me, not only questioning.”
  • OwnerThe biggest failure mode is B — the PM becomes a manual sync layer between the plan and where work actually happens. The tool assumes the PM is its only user.
  • CoachThen invert the direction of travel. Don't drag the team to the plan: let the tool read real signals — commits, ticket transitions, messages — and propose plan deltas. The PM shifts from typist to approver.
  • OwnerConcretely: plans live in MS Project, get manually pushed out to Jira / Backlog / Asana, then manually pulled back to measure progress and cost. A round trip. And a different tracker per project.
  • OwnerJudgement on the return trip is unavoidable, not optional. Worse: trackers accumulate many tasks that were never in the plan. The two sides don't drift — they structurally diverge.
  • OwnerAnd it's done inconsistently. Sometimes emergent tasks get added back into MS Project — which wrecks the baseline and cascades into downstream scheduling. Sometimes they're silently omitted.
  • OwnerThe hidden rule is task size. Work judged too small to bother recording gets silently omitted.
  • CoachThat omission is not random noise — it is a one-directional bias. Actuals are always understated, never overstated.

The loop that keeps estimates wrong forever

Capturing a 20-minute task must cost under 20 seconds — or the bias comes straight back. The first hard design constraint of the session, derived rather than asserted. Everything later about auto-linked evidence descends from this one number.
PLATE 02Technique · One Feature Only

Monday, 09:00

If the product could ship exactly one screen, what would it be? The answer arrived already structured — three tiers, in descending order of certainty.

  • OwnerThe one feature is the plan-vs-reality reconciliation map. (The coach's bet, endorsed rather than countered — worth noting, given what happens in Plate 07.)
  • OwnerBut I want to come back later to the classic set: WBS, Gantt, Resource, Calendar, Cost.
  • OwnerThe Monday-morning screen has three tiers: (1) how far the project has actually run, (2) where it is measurably diverging, (3) where it might be diverging — suspected, not yet proven.

WP-1165 — widest three-point spread in the plan signal: spread 3–21 d

Tier 3 states suspicion, never accusation. Weights start from PMI priors and are retrained by what actually goes wrong here.

  • OwnerTier-3 suspicion signals: (a) long-duration tasks whose reported progress is non-linear; (b) no visible output or deliverable at the milestones.
  • CoachThose two signals are one disease: progress asserted without evidence. The ‘% complete’ field is the vulnerability — a self-reported number with nothing attached. PMI already prescribes the cure (8/80 work-package sizing, deliverable-based 0/100 progress); MS Project lets you ignore both. So: never accept naked % complete. Show “verified by evidence” and “claimed, unverified” as visually distinct things.
  • OwnerIn this domain the output document is the evidence — design docs, minutes, research reports, specs cover nearly every task type.
  • CoachGaming risk: a tool that demands evidence gets fake documents — paperwork theatre, the exact thing that makes PMI hated.
  • OwnerEveryone on the team should be able to see the divergence screen. But it must be configurable.
  • CoachConfigurability doesn't remove the political dynamic, it relocates it to whoever configures. The default setting is the product's real opinion — default-open is radical transparency, with fear and gaming as its risk; default-PM-only is back to MS Project's single-user assumption, which is the original disease.
The tool never blocks and never punishes. It only makes truth visible. Absence of evidence is displayed as information, not as a violation. Corollary, straight out of Plate 01: attaching evidence must be near-automatic — the tool watches repo, drive and tracker and links artifacts itself — or the 20-second constraint breaks and the bias returns.
PLATE 03Technique · Assumption Reversal

The reversal that failed — and what fell out of it

The page inverts here because the technique did. The proposed reversal was rejected outright within one exchange; the value came from what the rejection exposed.

Rejected · unworkable

“What if the team authored the plan instead of the PM? Bottom-up planning, the PM as editor.”

Owner: In our reality the PM must always author the plan. Bottom-up planning is not available.

But the rejection was itself the discovery. Five moves followed, each one narrowing:

  1. If the PM is the sole author, nobody else committed to anything. Coach. Therefore divergence is not a verdict on people — it is a measurement of the plan's accuracy. That defuses the visibility politics from Plate 02, and yields a feature: score estimate accuracy per task type over time. “Design tasks run 40% under, consistently.”
  2. The PM-authors-the-plan rule is perception, not contract. Owner. The normal flow is: PM drafts, then ratifies with management and with the team.
  3. The ratification meeting already exists and leaves no durable trace. Coach. Capture it. Each member confirms their own packages in one click, with optional dissent — “I think this is underestimated.” Bottom-up signal without changing who authors the plan.
  4. Dissent happens, but the culture is top-down, so it is sparse. Owner. Some object, some correct. Not many.
  5. Sparse because expensive — so don't raise its volume, lower its cost. Coach. Replace the binary objection with PMI three-point estimating. The spread is dissent in a form nobody can take offence at: quantitative, so it feeds Tier 3 directly, and PMI-canonical, so it fits the standardise-by-PMI goal exactly.
  • OwnerObjectors are sometimes right and sometimes wrong. Dissent alone is not a reliable predictor.
  • CoachThe biggest idea in the session: nobody knows the hit rate because nobody ever measured it. So don't hard-code which Tier-3 signals predict failure. Record every signal — long task, non-linear progress, missing deliverable, wide spread, recorded dissent — and let outcomes score them over time. The product becomes a signal-calibration engine tuned to this specific organisation.
  • CoachDanger: per-person calibration (“A is right 80% of the time”) is a credibility score on humans — the surveillance risk returning worse. Default to scoring the signal; keep any person-level calibration private to that person.
PLATE 04Technique · Values Archaeology

Digging for the why

Why PMI, of all things? Four strikes of the spade. The first two answers were refused; the third held; the fourth turned out to be commercially load-bearing.

And then the goal itself reversed

As briefed, at the top of the session

For my own team first. Sell it later.

As corrected, in Plate 04

A product built to sell. Using it on my own team is dogfooding — validation, not the goal.

Owner: the opening framing was the permission structure, not the real order. — The first of two self-corrections; the second one, in Plate 07, is larger.

  • CoachConsequence of that reversal: the calibration engine is a moat for long-time users but has a cold-start problem for every new customer. Day one there is no history, so every Tier-3 signal is unweighted. PMI is what fills the gap before a customer's own data exists — ship PMI's priors as the starting weights, then let each org's outcomes retrain them. The academic motive becomes load-bearing rather than decorative.
  • OwnerOpen question. I cannot yet name a feature I would refuse to build even if it sold well. The truth-over-comfort value is visible all through this session but it is untested.

PLATE 07The course correction · owner-initiated

The session had been designing the wrong size of thing

The MoSCoW was finished. It looked complete. Then the owner read it back and noticed what was missing — and, more usefully, noticed why it was missing.

  • OwnerCorrection to the MoSCoW. Core PM capability is simply absent: task management, scheduling, resource management, cost management — and program / portfolio management across multiple projects. Portfolio is entirely new to this session.
  • CoachConceded: the MoSCoW silently assumed a companion product — an add-on that sits beside MS Project — without ever declaring that assumption. The opening brief said replacement.

The classic set is not table stakes to defer. It is the product. The reconciliation map is the differentiator built on it — not instead of it.

What the session had been building
MS PROJECT — still the system of record
WBSGanttResourceCalendarCost
Reconciliation map — an integration layer bolted to the side

Customer still pays for, and still works in, the tool they wanted to leave.

What the brief actually asked for

The map is one capability inside the product. It is what makes the product worth switching to — it is not the product.

How the option space got narrowed — at turn one, before any choice was made

This is the owner's own diagnosis of the session, not the coach's. It is the most valuable thing in the log, because it is reusable.

01Failure Analysis anchors on wound-fixing. The technique asks what hurts. It does not ask what is absent.
02The wound was tool-to-tool friction. So the answers were all about the seam between two tools, never about either tool's own job.
03All three opening sparks sat on the same plan-vs-reality axis. The field was one vein wide before a single idea was chosen.
04One Feature Only then forced a single pick from that already-narrow field. A convergence technique applied to a field that had never properly diverged.
  • OwnerArchitectural commitment. The data model must support multi-project and multi-department from day one. The portfolio UI can come later; the model cannot.
  • CoachMulti-department reopens the visibility decision from Plate 02. The hierarchy becomes tenant › department › program/portfolio › project › work package. Resource is the hard centre: a person belongs to a department but works across projects, so cross-project capacity contention is a core problem, not a later feature. Cost must roll up on two axes from day one — by project, and by department as cost centre. And visibility stops being a setting: department scoping is structural, so “everyone sees everything” only ever holds within a scope.
  • CoachGap for a future session. This session produced zero ideas on how the core PM capability beats MS Project — scheduling intelligence, resource optimisation, portfolio and department views, cost modelling. One vein mined deep; three untouched. The reconciliation map is not wrong. It is mis-sized.
PLATE 08Closing synthesis

What connects to what

Four links that none of the seven techniques produced on its own. Each one only exists because two separate moments in the session turned out to be the same moment.

01

“Too small to record”, “sometimes I just omit it”, and unattributed rework are three faces of one problem: invisible work — and all three are biased in the same direction.

work too small to record
Plate 01
inconsistent return trip
Plate 01
rework never attributed
Plate 05
Actuals understated · never overstated · never self-correcting

Three techniques, three hours apart, describing one mechanism. If the product fixes only one of the three, the number on the screen is still a lie — just a smaller one.

02

The academic motive for choosing PMI turned out to be the commercial answer to the cold-start problem.

Values Archaeology dug down to “personal experience is too small a sample to generalise from.” Two moves later, the calibration engine hit exactly the same wall on day one of every new customer: no history, nothing to generalise from. PMI's priors fill the gap until the customer's own outcomes take over. The research stops being a credential and becomes a component.

03

Disease and cure share a shape. MS Project assumes the PM is its only user — and so does this product. The difference is that this one travels to the team instead of summoning them.

Plate 01 named single-user-assumption as the original sin. Plate 05 then adopted it deliberately: full UI for the PM, read-only digests everywhere else, zero learning curve for anyone but the buyer. The same architecture, inverted in direction of travel. Worth watching: an inverted disease is still recognisably the disease.

04

“Where it might be diverging” produced Tier 3. “Objectors are sometimes right, sometimes wrong” produced the calibration engine that makes Tier 3 trustworthy. Neither survives alone.

A suspicion list with hard-coded weights is a superstition generator. A calibration engine with nothing to calibrate is an empty database. The owner supplied the first half; the coach supplied the second; the pair is the only defensible part of the whole design.

The product is not a planner. It is an instrument for measuring the distance between what a plan claims and what reality did — housed inside a planner that has to be good enough to replace the one they have.
