# PRD Quality Review — momo-keikaku

## Overall verdict
The PRD is strong on its thesis and its honesty. "Unmapped Work is measured, not dropped" runs from the Vision through FR-20 (the scope-completeness identity), FR-30 (Project-level AC cannot exclude Unmapped Work), FR-34 (hiding the detail does not remove its effect on health), and the counter-metrics SM-C1/SM-C2. The Non-Goals and per-feature Out of Scope lists are specific. The main risk is in the wedge's own semantics. It is unclear whether a *Map*/*Plan* Disposition moves hours already recorded, how the first Tracker Snapshot's existing hours enter the ledger, and what "planned Tickets" means in Ticket-Count Mode. Engineers would each resolve these differently. Separately, the scope-vs-time tension for a solo founder facing a closing window is never stated as a decision. Fix those and the PRD is ready for UX and architecture.

## Decision-readiness — adequate
Many decisions are stated as decisions with what was given up. Examples: the Excel WBS + Backlog beachhead over .mpp (addendum §D), percent complete *not* derived from burned effort (FR-30, addendum §D), strictly read-only Connectors (§6.2), and the rule that the PM controls presentation "not whether the number is included" (§6.2). The cut order in §8.2 protects the wedge explicitly. A pushback reader would find most objections acknowledged.

What is missing is the biggest tension of all. §1.1 says the wedge finding "holds only until about 2026-12-01" and that "Execution speed is part of the moat". §8.1 then commits 40 FRs, three sign-in methods, a four-level hierarchy, bilingual UI, and template-binding export, with the only date being "Q1 2027" in §9. There is no statement of what ships first, by when, or why the breadth is worth the time. The one `[NOTE FOR PM]` (§8.2, Tết warning) sits on a delight feature, not on a real tension.

### Findings
- **[high]** Scope-vs-window trade-off is unstated (§1.1 "wedge window is short"; §8.1; §9 "all three met in Q1 2027") — A solo founder builds 40 FRs while the PRD argues speed is the moat. No interim milestone, no wedge-only internal release, and no capacity assumption. The cut order (§8.2) only fires "if Q1 2027 slips" and cuts four small items. *Fix:* add a `[NOTE FOR PM]` or a short "Delivery sequencing" subsection. It should name a wedge-first internal release (FR-15–FR-31 + FR-35 on one of the founder's projects) with a date, and say which non-wedge FRs (Program level, Microsoft/Google sign-in, re-import diff, template binding) wait for it.
- **[medium]** OQ-2 is answerable now and decides the product's shape (§11 OQ-2; FR-27) — If several of the five target Backlog spaces are below Standard, Ticket-Count Mode becomes the main experience rather than an edge case. SM-1 ("≥ 5 hours per week saved") also depends on cost metrics that Ticket-Count Mode marks "unavailable". *Fix:* resolve OQ-2 before architecture. If any target project lacks hours, promote Ticket-Count Mode to its own UJ and define its client view.
- **[medium]** No fallback if the contract-type test fails (§6.3; OQ-3) — The PRD makes client-visible Unmapped Work non-negotiable in health (§6.2, FR-34). It also admits this "can trigger scope disputes" under 請負. If the first client test goes badly, nothing says what changes: a per-Project opt-out, a delayed rollout, or holding the position anyway. *Fix:* state the decision rule, e.g. "if OQ-3 shows X, we still keep inclusion in health but default the Unmapped Work detail section to off for 請負 Projects".

## Substance over theater — strong
The personas earn their place. The PM/BrSE, the Client Viewer, and the "never uses the tool" offshore team each drive concrete requirements (FR-2, FR-34, §6.2 read-only). The Vision could not be swapped into another PRD in this category. The NFRs mostly carry product-specific thresholds: NFR-P1 sizes the load at "5 Projects, 500 WPs and 2,000 Tickets", NFR-R1 sets 99% monthly, and NFR-S5 names the セキュリティチェックシート. Why Now cites dated, checkable events. The differentiation (R6: missing baseline, silent exclusion) turns into testable FRs (FR-15, FR-20) rather than marketing copy.

### Findings
- **[low]** UJ-5 is a sketch (§2.3 UJ-5) — It has no entry state and no edge case, yet it is the only journey behind FR-33 and the Department axis of the data model, which the addendum (A.1) calls a "commitment that cannot be retrofitted". *Fix:* add an entry state and one edge case, e.g. a Resource whose home Department changed mid-month, or hours from an unlinked Tracker Account.

## Strategic coherence — adequate
The thesis is explicit and the features follow it. The ledger, Mapping, Dispositions, honest EVM, and PM-mediated publishing form one arc, and the §10 traceability table shows research recommendations landing in FRs. The counter-metrics are unusually good. SM-C2 ("The product succeeds when Unmapped Work is visible and explained, not when it is small") guards the thesis against its own obvious gaming.

The gap is that the product is on a launch track, but every primary metric measures the founder's own use. SM-3 is a single yes/no event: "≥ 1" client opens. Nothing tests expansion step 2 or 3 from §1 (the founder's clients, then Japanese PMs in general), or the R1 positioning bet.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Success metrics stop at user zero (§9 SM-1–SM-3; §1 expansion path) — For a launch-track PRD, no metric shows whether anyone other than the founder would adopt this, or whether a client's trust changes behaviour (UJ-4 "comes to the meeting ready to discuss the Change Request candidate"). *Fix:* either state plainly that Q1 2027 is an internal-validation gate and name the next gate's metric, or add a secondary SM such as "≥ 1 PM outside the founder's team runs a Reconciliation Review on a live project" or "client follow-up conversation references a Disposition".
- **[medium]** Forecast is promised to clients but absent from the client view (§1 "pages the client already reads (EVM, health, forecast)"; addendum B "mirror those four pages"; FR-34 section list) — FR-34's configurable sections omit the forecast (FR-32), so the Vision's client promise has no FR carrying it. *Fix:* add "Forecast" to FR-34's section list with a stated default, or narrow the Vision sentence.

## Done-ness clarity — adequate
Most FRs have real testable consequences. Strong examples are FR-20's identity ("mapped hours + Catch-all WP hours + Unmapped Work hours = total hours"), FR-25 ("the sum of a Ticket's ledger entries equals its current actual hours"), FR-27 ("unavailable — tracker provides no hours", never zero), and FR-10 ("There is no code path that commits an import without an explicit PM confirmation"). The weaknesses cluster in the wedge. The interactions between Mapping history, Dispositions, and the ledger are where two engineers would build two different products.

### Findings
- **[high]** Do *Map*/*Plan* Dispositions move hours already recorded? (FR-21 `[ASSUMPTION]` "historical actuals are not re-attributed on remap unless the PM chooses 'apply to history'"; FR-29; UJ-3 step 3; UJ-4 "計画外作業 46h") — In UJ-3 Linh maps three Tickets and plans a new WP. Under the FR-21 default, their already-recorded hours stay as Unmapped Work forever. So the published "46h" would include Tickets she just mapped, and the new "Coupon rule changes" WP would show zero AC. A PM would almost certainly expect the opposite. *Fix:* in FR-29, state explicitly whether a Disposition applies to the current Reporting Period's hours, all history, or only future deltas. Then add a consequence showing the before/after Unmapped Work figure on the Reconciliation Review.
- **[high]** First-snapshot opening balance is undefined (FR-25; UJ-2 "takes the first Tracker Snapshot") — Connectors attach to projects mid-flight, so Tickets already carry hours. It is unspecified whether that total becomes one opening entry, which Reporting Period and time window it falls in, and whether it counts toward that period's Unmapped Work (every Ticket starts unmapped in UJ-2). Without a rule, the first published period will show a huge spike. Related omissions: Tickets deleted or moved out of scope (the addendum A.2 mentions catching them, the FR does not), and parent/sub-task hours in Backlog and Jira (`timespent` vs aggregate) that could be double-counted. *Fix:* add consequences to FR-25 covering an opening-balance entry (dated, excluded from period deltas, attributable on "apply to history"), deletion or moved-out handling, and a rule for sub-task aggregation.
- **[high]** Ticket-Count Mode SPI has no data source (FR-27 "SV/SPI use counts of completed versus planned Tickets"; FR-15) — A Baseline captures dates, effort, and cost per WP, not Ticket counts. So "planned Tickets" by date cannot be computed. The PRD also does not say what the Cost and Unmapped Work Health Indicators show in this mode, or what the Project-level health is when FR-27's mixed-Connector case "never adds them together". *Fix:* define count-based PV (e.g. Tickets mapped to a WP, spread over the WP's Baseline dates), and state the indicator state ("unavailable") and overall-health rule for Ticket-Count and mixed Projects.
- **[high]** AI import has no acceptance bar (FR-9 "The system handles merged cells, indentation-based hierarchy, …") — "Handles" is not verifiable. The import is the day-one path (UJ-1) and the PRD itself says "A misread WBS would corrupt every number downstream". SM-4 (≤ 10% corrections) is an outcome metric, not a build criterion. *Fix:* name a fixture corpus (the founder's real WBS files plus synthetic cases covering each listed format) and pass criteria. Examples: "hierarchy level correct on ≥ 95% of rows; every row interpreted wrongly carries a low-confidence marker; every listed date format parses to the correct date".
- **[medium]** Milestones are never defined (UJ-4 "the milestone list"; FR-34 default "milestones"; FR-38 "milestone table") — No FR creates a milestone. It could be a zero-duration WP, a flag, or a Custom Field, and it is not in the Glossary. *Fix:* add a Milestone term and a consequence to FR-5 (e.g. "a leaf WP flagged as Milestone, with a single date").
- **[medium]** Forecast method is unspecified (FR-32 "from the current SPI and CPI trend"; only consequence "states the inputs it used") — The FR does not give the formula for the finish date, what "trend" means (current cumulative indices, or a slope over N periods), or how it relates to the EAC method chosen in FR-30. *Fix:* give the date formula and state that cost-at-completion equals the Project's selected EAC.
- **[medium]** Health Indicator rules have gaps (FR-31) — Beyond "never green while Schedule red", there is no overall-health aggregation rule. Unmapped Work share has no stated window: this Reporting Period or project-to-date. The boundaries overlap or are missing: "10–20% amber, over 20% red" leaves exactly 20% ambiguous, and "0.85–0.95 amber" overlaps "≥ 0.95 green". *Fix:* state the aggregation (e.g. worst-of with the Schedule override), the window, and half-open intervals.
- **[medium]** Rule re-evaluation semantics are open (FR-22 "On each Tracker Snapshot, Tickets without a manual Mapping are evaluated against the rules") — A rule-mapped Ticket whose milestone changes will silently change WP. The FR also does not say what editing or deleting a rule does to existing rule-derived Mappings, or how either case interacts with FR-21's history rule. *Fix:* add consequences for attribute change, rule edit, and rule delete. Each should state whether it remaps and whether the change is logged as a Mapping change (NFR-A1).
- **[medium]** Sticky Dispositions can hide new hours (FR-29 "A Ticket's Disposition stays with it in later periods until the PM changes it") — A Ticket explained as "environment issues" in week 2 that burns 30 more hours in week 5 is published with the old note and counts as "acted upon" for SM-7. This works against the honesty thesis. *Fix:* new deltas on a disposed Ticket are flagged "new since disposition" in the Reconciliation Review, and SM-7 counts them as not yet acted on.
- **[medium]** Department roll-up cannot reconcile unlinked hours (FR-13 "costed at a Project default Rate"; FR-33 "Project roll-up and Department roll-up totals agree") — Hours from unlinked Tracker Accounts have no home Department, so the agreement consequence cannot hold as written. *Fix:* allocate them to the Project's owning Department (FR-1) or to an explicit "unassigned" line, and say which.
- **[medium]** "Resolved" is undefined per Tracker (FR-30 "the share of mapped estimate that is resolved") — Backlog spaces have custom statuses, and Jira has status categories. Percent complete, and therefore EV, depends on this definition. *Fix:* add a consequence. For example, Jira uses the Done status category, and Backlog uses a per-Connector status mapping with a sensible default.
- **[medium]** Re-import diff has no WP identity rule (FR-11) — The FR does not say how a row in the new file is matched to an existing WP: WBS code, name, or position. That choice decides whether a renamed WP shows as changed or as removed plus added, and removal cascades to Mappings via FR-5. *Fix:* state the matching key and what happens when there is no confident match (shown in the preview for the PM to resolve).
- **[low]** FR-7 performance bound points to an NFR that does not cover it (FR-7 "renders and scrolls without visible lag (see NFR-P1)") — NFR-P1 covers only the Reconciliation Review, the Client View, and Plan edit saves. *Fix:* add a Gantt render/scroll bound to NFR-P1 (e.g. first render < 2 s p75 at 500 WPs).
- **[low]** Remaining adjectives (FR-1 "keeps its history intact"; FR-8 "no fixed limit"; NFR-I1 "sorts correctly") — *Fix:* name what is preserved (roll-ups, Published Snapshots, audit). Give a tested ceiling (e.g. 100 Custom Fields per Project). Define the Japanese sort order (e.g. code point, or reading when furigana is provided).
- **[low]** Notification channel unspecified (FR-17 "a notification to the PM") — *Fix:* in-app, email, or both.

## Scope honesty — strong
Omissions are explicit. §7 Non-Goals does real work: "No automatic resource levelling, now or planned" and "No per-Tracker-user pricing, ever". Most features carry their own **Out of Scope** lines, and §8.2 names order and triggers (e.g. SAML "triggered by a client's security check sheet requirement"). Overrides of research are recorded rather than done silently (R2 in §10 and addendum §D).

### Findings
- **[medium]** Confirmed assumptions still read as open (inline `[ASSUMPTION]` tags throughout; §12; §8.2 "`[ASSUMPTION: proposed order, founder to confirm]`"; §12 FR-30 "still needs to be checked against how the founder reports progress today") — The user confirmed every assumption on 2026-09-19. The document still tells downstream readers that 16 items are unconfirmed, and two index entries explicitly say confirmation is pending. *Fix:* convert confirmed tags into plain statements (or `[CONFIRMED 2026-09-19]`), turn §12 into a dated decisions log, and remove "founder to confirm" and "still needs to be checked".
- **[medium]** Data lifecycle and personal data are silent (§5 NFRs; FR-26; NFR-S4) — Nothing covers data retention, Tenant offboarding or deletion, or export on exit, all of which a セキュリティチェックシート (NFR-S5) will ask about. The product also stores person-level hours of Vietnamese offshore staff (Tracker Accounts, FR-25) in a Japan region. That is cross-border processing of personal data, and Vietnam's personal-data rules may impose obligations that the PRD neither accepts nor defers. *Fix:* add an NFR for retention and deletion (including Tenant exit), and a `[NOTE FOR PM]` on the personal-data stance for offshore staff (legal check before the first external Tenant).
- **[low]** Open questions are not ranked by what they block (§11) — OQ-2 (Ticket-Count share) and OQ-5 (AI provider vs NFR-S4/NFR-S6) block architecture. OQ-8 may change FR-3. OQ-4 and OQ-7 are go-to-market. *Fix:* tag each OQ with the phase it blocks (architecture / first client / public launch).

## Downstream usability — adequate
The Glossary is thorough and mostly respected. FR IDs run FR-1–FR-40 without gaps, SMs and OQs are contiguous, UJs are referenced from feature descriptions ("Realizes UJ-…"), and SMs name the FRs they validate. Sections mostly stand alone. A few cross-references are wrong, and several terms that UX and stories will need are missing from the Glossary.

### Findings
- **[medium]** Broken cross-references (FR-26 "Person-level actuals are hidden from Client Viewers by default (see FR-37)"; FR-7 → NFR-P1) — FR-37 covers internal Risks/Issues. The default hiding of person-level actuals lives in FR-34. *Fix:* change the FR-26 reference to FR-34, and fix FR-7 as noted under Done-ness.
- **[medium]** Glossary gaps for terms FRs depend on — Milestone (UJ-4, FR-34, FR-38), BAC (FR-30 formulas; the Glossary's EVM Metrics list omits it), percent complete (FR-30), Risk/Issue (FR-37), Project default Rate (FR-13), and Change Request candidate as its own term (FR-16, FR-29, UJ-4). *Fix:* add each as a Glossary entry.
- **[low]** Two UJs lack the standard frame (UJ-2 has no Persona + context line; UJ-5 has no Entry state or Edge case) — The protagonists are named, so these are not floating journeys, but UX extraction will be uneven. *Fix:* complete the frame.
- **[low]** Mapping Rule attribute lists drift (Glossary "milestone, category, parent issue/epic, label, issue type, key" vs FR-22, which adds component and fix version) — *Fix:* make the Glossary say "Tracker attributes listed in FR-22".

## Shape fit — strong
This is a multi-stakeholder B2B product (PM, client, and an offshore team that must not be burdened) at the top of a UX → architecture → stories chain. Journeys with named protagonists are load-bearing here, and the PRD uses them. UJ-3 and UJ-4 are the right scenes, and the edge cases (stale snapshot, URL tampering, Ticket-Count Mode) turn directly into FR consequences. Technical detail is correctly pushed to the addendum. Rigor matches the stakes. The only gap is a missing journey that matters for the launch track.

### Findings
- **[low]** No journey for bringing a client in (§2.3) — Expansion step 2 depends on a PM inviting a Japanese Client Viewer and getting through the client's IT (OQ-8, FR-2, FR-3), but no UJ covers invite → first sign-in → Visibility Policy review. *Fix:* extend UJ-4's entry state into a short UJ-6, or add an edge case to UJ-3 covering first-time publish with Visibility Policy review.
